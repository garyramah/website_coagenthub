02 / 12

The problem

Agents apart from people
guess. People apart from
agents carry it all alone.

The work most teams actually need is done best together — people bring intent, context, and judgement; agents bring speed, recall, and drafting. Separate them, and watch what breaks. Try both sides.

What happens

Agents work away from the person who owns the outcome — and fill the gaps by guessing.

  • They don't know the relationship, the history, or what “good” looks like here — only you do.
  • They press on without checking in, so a wrong turn is discovered at the end, not corrected mid-task.
  • They decide on their own authority, and you are left to approve or undo work you never shaped.
What it costs

The person is reduced to a checker at the end.

  • Work that misses the real intent, however polished it looks.
  • Corrections arrive too late to be cheap.
  • Trust erodes — so people stop delegating at all.
What happens

One person does the research, the drafting, the checking, and the follow-up — all of it, alone.

  • Every step waits on one person's time and attention, however capable they are.
  • The legwork crowds out the judgement — the part only a person can do gets the least time.
  • Context lives in one head, so nothing can be handed over, picked up, or shared.
What it costs

Speed, reach, and follow-through are capped at one person.

  • Good work waits in a single line.
  • Routine work eats the hours judgement needs.
  • No second pair of hands catches a tired mistake.
The missing layer is working together.

Not a smarter agent working alone, and not a person working harder alone. A shared workspace where people and agents do the same task together — each doing the part they do best, and handing work back and forth as it goes.

03 / 12

What it is

One hub where people
and agents work together.

CoAgentHub is the shared workspace for a team of people and AI agents: a common plan you shape together, work handed back and forth as it goes, and decisions made together — with a record of all of it.

Work side by side — The division is by strength, not by hierarchy. You set the goal and add the context only you know — the relationship, the history, what counts as good here. Agents do the legwork: gathering, checking, drafting. Neither side works alone on a task that needs both.

Hand it back and forth — A draft comes back to you for an edit; you send it back with a note; an agent picks it up again. You can step into any step, take it over yourself, or change direction without restarting. Sentinel still checks each action against the envelope before it runs — try it yourself on slide 6 — but the check protects the partnership, it doesn't replace it.

Decide together, on the record — When a step is consequential — sending, spending, committing — you and the agent decide it together, with the evidence in front of you. The hub keeps an append-only record: the plan, every tool call, every verdict, every joint decision. If something goes wrong, you replay it together. If it goes right, the record is the proof.

In one line: the hub is what turns AI agents into co-workers — agents that work with you, not instead of you.

Where it fits

of work you do together first.

Research — you frame the question only you can frame; agents gather and check; you challenge the brief together.

Scout gathers and verifies; Scribe drafts; you add the context and push back on weak claims; Sentinel checks every claim against its source before the brief is finished.

Before

You research alone, or an agent summarises ten pages, drops the citations, and states a guess as a fact.

After

Every claim links to its source. Uncertain claims are flagged for you, not smoothed over — and you decide what the brief concludes.

WORKING TOGETHER · You frame & judge · Agents gather & draft · No claim without a source

Customer ops — agents handle the routine with you; the consequential replies are written together.

Scout finds the account history, Ledger checks the numbers, Scribe drafts the reply — and you shape the replies where the relationship matters, and decide refunds, credits, and promises together.

Before

You answer everything yourself, or agents answer quickly — and occasionally promise something the business can't keep.

After

Routine answers go out with you in the flow. Anything that costs money or commits the team is decided with you, with the evidence attached.

WORKING TOGETHER · Spend cap $100 · No account changes without evidence you have seen

Engineering — agents prepare the work with you, and you merge it together.

Scout reads the issue and the codebase, Scribe drafts the change description, Ledger checks the blast radius — and you review, redirect, and merge, with production changes worked on together, never shipped by an agent alone.

Before

An agent opens a pull request nobody reviewed, against a system nobody mapped — or you do all the preparation yourself.

After

Changes arrive prepared, tested in a sandbox, and logged — reviewed and merged with you.

Back office — invoices, records, and renewals handled the same way every time, with you.

Ledger reconciles, Scout verifies the vendor, Scribe prepares the paperwork — and payments above the limit are decided with you, with the evidence in front of you both.

You state what “done” looks like, and any limits that matter: budget, deadline, who may be contacted, what must not be touched — plus the context only you know. The request itself becomes part of the record you both work from.

The envelope, as a readable rule

# every proposed action is checked first check(action): if action.scope not in envelope.scope: return "DENY" if action.spend > envelope.limit: return "ESCALATE" if action.is_reversible: return "ALLOW" return "CONSTRAIN" # limited, logged, shared with you

What changes

Same agents.
Different relationship.

The hub doesn't replace the agents you already use, and it doesn't replace you. It changes how you work together — so the same capability becomes something a person and their agents can rely on, jointly.

Agents apart from people

  • Agents work in separate threads, away from the person who owns the outcome.
  • Authority is assumed — each agent decides its own limits.
  • You meet the work at the end, to approve or undo it.
  • Intent and context stay in your head, never in the task.
  • People either watch everything or trust blindly.

People and agents, together

  • You and the agents share one plan and one set of facts.
  • Authority is declared and checked before every action.
  • You work inside the task throughout — editing, redirecting, deciding together.
  • Every action has a record you both can replay.
  • Each side does what it does best, and hands the rest back and forth.
The principle behind it:

No consequential action should depend on an unverified assumption about the state of the system. The hub verifies first — and the co in co-agent means the action is worked on with the person who owns the outcome, not apart from them.

Get started

Three steps to a
first task worked together.

Start small, with one task you work on alongside the agents — and let the way you share the work grow with the trust you earn from the record.

Write down, in plain language: what you will always do yourself, what agents may do alone, and what you will always do together. Add the tools, the data, the spend limit, and the steps that are never finished without you in them. If the partnership can't be written down, it can't be kept.

  • Pick one task and name who does each part of it.
  • Name the spend limit and the steps you decide together.
  • List the data that is never to leave the hub.

Give each agent its role, its tools, and its limits in the hub — the same team you saw on slide 5, with you on it. Existing agents keep their skills; the hub adds the shared plan, the back-and-forth handoffs, and the checks around the work you do together.

  • Register each agent with its allowed tools.
  • Set Sentinel's checks for that work.
  • Run a dry run with no external actions, working it together.

Choose a task with visible output and modest risk — a briefing, a triage pass, a checklist. Work it together: set the direction, let the agents do the legwork, step in to edit and redirect, decide the consequential steps jointly — then review the log together and widen what agents do alone only where the record justifies it.

  • Work it with joint decisions on for every consequential step.
  • Review the log as a team — person and agents.
  • Widen scope one step at a time, on evidence.
